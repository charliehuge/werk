Push-up with the hands elevated on a box, bench, or wall. Raising the hands
offloads bodyweight, letting the same full-range mechanics — braced trunk,
elbows tucked, chest to the surface — be trained before a floor push-up is
accessible. The higher the hands, the easier: wall is the friendliest entry,
dropping to a low box as strength builds.

Cues: hands just wider than shoulders, whole body one straight line from ear to
heel, elbows drive back at ~45° (not flared wide), touch the chest to the edge,
press the surface away and finish tall through the mid-back.

Common faults: hips sagging or piking, head craning down instead of leading with
the chest, elbows flaring to a T, partial range near the top.

Scaling: raise the hands higher to make it easier, lower them toward the floor to
make it harder — the ladder up to a full floor push-up.
