The floor push-up: a moving plank that presses. From a straight-line hold on the
hands, lower the whole body as one unit until the chest grazes the floor, then
press back to a locked-out, protracted top. Upper-body pushing strength welded to
a braced trunk — the trunk work is not optional, it's half the movement.

Cues: hands under the shoulders or just wider, squeeze glutes and brace the ribs
down so the body stays rigid, elbows back at ~45°, lead the descent with the
chest, lower to a floor touch, press away and reach the upper back to the ceiling
at the top.

Common faults: hips sagging (lost brace) or piking up, head dipping while the
chest stays high, elbows flaring wide, bottoming out with only a half rep.

Scaling: regress to an [Incline Push-Up](incline-push-up.md) — the higher the
hands the easier — or drop to the knees keeping the same straight torso. Progress
by slowing the eccentric, pausing at the bottom, or elevating the feet.
